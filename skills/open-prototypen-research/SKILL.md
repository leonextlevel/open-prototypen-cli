# Research

Choose the research artifact that resolves a real decision. Read its CLI instructions and dependencies first. Seek current primary sources when possible; record URLs, access dates, observations, and implications. Distinguish facts, claims, interpretations, and assumptions. Do not present invented users, revenue, market size, or conversion metrics as evidence.

For UX research, start from the pattern decisions the product faces, and weigh sources by kind: official design-system guidance, established UX or HCI research, and mature real products carry more weight than specialized research, and visual inspiration is not usability evidence at all.

For visual research, look for references relevant to this product's audience, content, and task. Describe concrete choices in hierarchy, typography, color roles, spacing, alignment, components, states, and icons. Give each reference its own block with **Observed**, **Why relevant**, **Principle extracted**, and **Do not copy**, and synthesize principles across several references rather than from one. Record gaps or uncertain observations. References yield principles, not layouts to clone; never copy proprietary composition, distinctive component shapes, or brand identity. Validate each completed artifact.
